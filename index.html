The business view

MARKETING HAS
TWO BIG JOBS.

01 · Customer acquisition

Help the right people discover the business, understand the offer and become customers.

  • Content marketing and social media
  • Paid advertising and SEO
  • Influencer marketing, PR and partnerships
  • Sales outreach and referrals

02 · Customer retention

Give customers good reasons to stay, return, recommend and buy again.

  • Customer experience and support
  • Email, SMS and WhatsApp follow-up
  • Loyalty, personalisation and CRM
  • Community and re-engagement

A business needs both. The right balance depends on its stage, offer, customers and goals.

Two ways to build experience

WORK WITH A BUSINESS.
OR BUILD YOUR OWN.

Working with an existing business

  • Learn one function deeply inside a bigger operation.
  • See how teams, budgets, customers and goals connect.
  • Get feedback, collaboration and experience with real constraints.
  • Build evidence you can use for jobs or client work.

Marketing your own business

  • Apply several marketing functions at once.
  • Learn directly from your own customers and sales.
  • Make decisions quickly, often with limited resources.
  • Accept that you may be doing strategy, content, sales and support together.

Neither path is automatically better. Choose based on your goal, resources and the kind of experience you need.

Specialise, generalise, or combine11
Generalist or specialist?

YOU CAN START SMALL
AND EXPAND OVER TIME.

Specialist

Build depth in a particular problem or function.

Examples: SEO specialist, paid media buyer, email marketing specialist, brand strategist, account manager.

Generalist

Coordinate multiple marketing functions and understand how they work together.

Examples: Marketing executive, marketing manager, growth marketer at a small business.

You do not need to choose your forever role today. Choose the next skill or project that will teach you something useful.

Put the tool in context

MATCH THE TOOL
TO THE JOB.

Understand customers

Use Google Search, Google Trends and Google Forms to investigate questions, demand and customer needs.

Create content

Use Canva or CapCut to produce a useful asset for a defined audience and goal.

Measure results

Use Google Analytics 4 and Sheets to understand traffic, actions and patterns.

Find new customers

Use SEO, paid ads, partnerships or outreach depending on the audience and offer.

Bring customers back

Use email, CRM and WhatsApp Business to follow up appropriately and improve the experience.

Work with a team

Use shared documents and task boards to make responsibilities and deadlines visible.

A marketer who understands the customer and objective can learn a new platform more effectively than someone who only knows where to click.

THE SKILLS THAT
MAKE YOU STAND OUT.

Marketing knowledge matters. So does how you communicate, show your thinking, and keep improving. Select each quality to see what it looks like in practice.

01 · SHOW YOUR THINKING

Build a personal brand

Your personal brand is the evidence people build about how you think, what you care about, and the kind of work you can do.

Two ways to show it

Portfolio: show the problem, your thinking, the work and what you learned. Building in public: share useful observations, experiments, lessons and progress as you learn.

Why it helps

It gives employers and potential collaborators a way to discover your thinking. Some companies also look for employee-creators who can communicate authentically about their work and industry.

The bridge from learning to doing

WHY WORK WITH ME
FOR EIGHT WEEKS?

The apprenticeship is built around doing real marketing work with guidance, not collecting certificates or sitting through theory alone.

01 · Find your direction

Explore your strengths, understand business and audience needs, and choose a specialty direction to practise.

02 · Learn how growth works

Work through different forms of customer acquisition and understand how marketing connects to business goals.

03 · Build through projects

Follow a hands-on arc: Week 1 business and audience analysis; Week 2 content strategy and creative direction; Week 3 creative formats; Week 6 marketing execution, with practical work and feedback throughout.

04 · Get feedback

Share specialty-based projects, review your work with guidance, improve your decisions and learn how to explain the thinking behind your output.

05 · Build your portfolio

Turn selected work into evidence of what you can do, what problem you solved and what you learned.

06 · Prepare for opportunities

Develop practical experience that can support applications, client work, business growth or your next learning step. Employment or income is not guaranteed.

The idea: we learn, apply, share work, get feedback and improve together. The aim is to help you move from “I’m interested in marketing” to showing the kind of work you can contribute.
